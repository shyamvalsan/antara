Go Sun-deok dived off Hado-ri for sixty-one years. For fifty of them she painted in ink on the backs of cooperative ledgers on winter evenings, and showed nobody.

Photographs of the diving women show the women. She paints what the women saw. A sheet is one dive, with a way down and a way up, and more paper than ink, because the paper is the air. The only colour is her seal. It reads breath, and sits where her float would be.

The maker

Hado-ri, Jeju, born 1944

Her mother dived, and her grandmother before her. At eight she was practising in the shallows with a gourd float; at fifteen she went out with the women. A diver's whole skill, she says, is knowing to the second how much breath is left, and never taking the one last shell that would cost it.

An old schoolteacher in the next village taught the diving girls to write their names so that the cooperative could not cheat them. He saw how she held the brush and kept her on.

The cold finished her wrists in 2022. Her granddaughter, who builds models of the ocean at the university, sat with her through one winter and wrote down how she paints: how wet, how fast, where the brush lifts, how long to wait. Sun-deok does not touch the machine. She says what is wrong with the picture, and the numbers are changed. There is no white paint and no correction. If a sheet is wrong it is begun again.

Things Sun-deok has said

You do not look at the sea. You are in it or you are not.

The paper is the air. Do not spend it.

My hands are finished. My eyes went down for sixty years. Those still work.

One hook in this room is kept empty.
