Three pictures: four seconds, eleven and nine. Tobi Adeyemi is rider 417 for a dispatch company, and was three years into an architecture degree when the fees stopped.

He paints a junction the way he has to see it to get through it: ahead, both mirrors, a glance down, a glance up, all at once. Each piece of a picture is one of those looks. Somewhere in everything he makes there is a piece of bus yellow with two black stripes.

The maker

Lagos, born 1999

He was raised in Yaba by his mother, who sells cloth in Balogun market. At university he was good at the thing first-years find hardest: holding a plan, a section and an elevation in the head and knowing they are one object. Riding in Lagos turned out to be the same exercise at speed, with a hand's width on each side.

He went down once, at Ojuelegba in 2022, and broke a collarbone. In the six weeks off the bike he began to draw what he had seen in the seconds before.

He builds each junction as a rough model, takes his views through it, and cuts the panel along lines that are already there: a kerb, a wire, the edge of a bus. The surface is sign-painter's enamel and car spray on salvaged plywood. He learned lettering from the men at the bus park, who can write a slogan on a bus that is moving. He signs with his rider number.
