Luz Bautista answers telephones for nine hours a night under another name. Then she has ninety minutes on a bus, and these pictures are those minutes.

Nothing in the city is in focus. She says that is the accurate report: tired eyes, wet glass, too many lights. In each picture exactly one drop of water is sharp, and the whole street is inside it, upside down. You will find it second.

The maker

Quezon City, born 2003

She was raised by her grandmother in Cubao. Her mother has nursed in Riyadh since Luz was six, and for most of her life has been a face on a screen, slightly delayed, lit from below. Luz says this is why soft light and glass feel like tenderness to her and not like distance.

She wanted to study film. At nineteen she took the call-centre job because the night shift paid more. She learned to write the programs that make these from videos played at three-quarter speed, on her breaks. Nothing is photographed. The lights are placed one at a time, and then the lens and the water are calculated.

She signs with a fingertip, in the condensation, lower right.

Things Luz has said

The phone focuses on the city or the glass. I am the glass.

Everyone awake at 4 am is working for someone who is asleep.

It's not sad. It's just the way home.

One hook in this room is kept empty.
