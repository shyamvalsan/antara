About Antara

Leela VarkeyDirector and curator

Leela Varkey was born in Fort Kochi in 1973. She trained as a paintings conservator in London and spent eight years at the bench, on panel paintings and then on South Asian manuscripts, before moving to curating. She was senior curator of a museum collection in Rotterdam and, from 2012 to 2024, chief curator of a private museum in Mumbai.

She founded Antara in 2026 to show a kind of work she could not place in a museum: pictures made entirely by written instruction, in which the maker specifies a world, its light and the hand that paints it. As a conservator she spent years reconstructing how painters built light in layers. Here those decisions are written down.

Antara means the interval between two things, and also what is within. She uses it for the distance between a person and a picture.

I am not interested in how it was made until I have forgotten to ask.

ColophonHow the gallery was made

Antara is a work of fiction. The gallery, its curator and the makers are invented, and so are their lives. Any resemblance to a real person is a coincidence. The places, trades and traditions the makers come from are real.

The pictures are real in the way the gallery says they are. Each is drawn by a single program written for that picture. No photographs, scans or other images go in. A place is built from geometry and lit, or a sheet of paper is wetted and inked, and the picture is what the program leaves.

The voice of the audio guide is synthesized. The gallery was made by a person and an AI model working together. The programme will change.
