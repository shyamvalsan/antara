About Antara

LeelaDirector and curator

Leela was born in Fort Kochi in 1973. She trained as a paintings conservator in London and spent eight years at the bench, on panel paintings and then on South Asian manuscripts, before moving to curating. She was senior curator of a museum collection in Rotterdam and, from 2012 to 2024, chief curator of a private museum in Mumbai.

She founded Antara in 2026 to show a kind of work she could not place in a museum: pictures made entirely by written instruction, in which the maker specifies a world, its light and the hand that paints it. As a conservator she spent years reconstructing how painters built light in layers. Here those decisions are written down.

Antara means the interval between two things, and also what is within. She uses it for the distance between a person and a picture.

I am not interested in how it was made until I have forgotten to ask.
A good room has one more wall than it has pictures.
Stand further back than you think. Then come much closer than is polite.

The plain factsWhat Antara is, and how it was made

Antara is a work of fiction from start to finish. The gallery, its curator, the six makers and their biographies, the exhibition texts, the building and every artwork were conceived and made by Fable 5.2, an AI model, as an experiment set going by one person. See The experimenter, below.

No image-generation model was used. No photographs were used, and no source images of any kind. Each work is one self-contained Python program, numerical code that builds geometry, traces light, and simulates ink and paint, and the program computes the image, pixel by pixel. The full source of each program is published beside its work, so you can read exactly how the picture was made.

The voice of the audio guide and the music are synthesized.

The places, trades and traditions the makers come from are real. The people are not. Any resemblance to a real person is a coincidence.

The experimenter

Behind the artificial mind there is a person. Shyam Sreevalsan set this experiment going: asked what a gallery would be if a machine imagined all of it, the people as well as the pictures; kept sending the work back until the pictures were worth standing in front of; and built Antara to share with the world.
